# LW2B Final Assessment

## Final status

LW2B is archived. The demo is complete.

## What worked well

### 1. Deterministic RTS core
The project established a credible deterministic RTS core with economy, production, movement, combat, and map play all functioning together.

### 2. Clear systems thinking
The game developed around readable systems rather than content sprawl:
- opening plans
- contested-resource pressure
- wood economy and Lumber Mill timing
- race identity
- doctrine and upgrade structure

### 3. Strong simulation and AI learning value
A large portion of the project's value is in the simulation and AI work:
- movement recovery and worker flow
- assault posture and pressure logic
- role split and initiative identity
- econ-collapse and endgame behavior
- regression-oriented validation surfaces

### 4. Useful networking experimentation
LW2B also produced valuable practical work around online transport support and operational topology thinking without requiring a server-authoritative redesign.

## Where the ceiling appeared

### 1. Limited content density
The project remained intentionally compact. That compactness is one of its strengths, but it also limits long-term gameplay variety.

### 2. Narrow army and tech vocabulary
The game has enough pieces for a good demo, but not enough gameplay vocabulary to sustain a much broader continuation through tuning alone.

### 3. Map diversity helps, but does not fully change match identity
The map pool improves pacing and route geometry, but does not by itself create enough distinct match archetypes to justify a much larger long-term expansion without a broader content phase.

### 4. Future growth would no longer be a small pass
The next meaningful increase in variety would require a larger design/content step, not just incremental balance or AI refinement.

## Why archiving is the right call

Archiving does not mean the project failed.

It means the project successfully completed its strongest version of the current phase:
- a compact experimental RTS demo
- technically meaningful
- design-informative
- playable enough to validate the main ideas

Continuing past this point would likely mean changing the nature of the project, not simply finishing a few obvious next steps.

## Reusable value for future projects

LW2B leaves behind useful reference material for future work in:
- deterministic simulation design
- RTS input/sim/net boundary thinking
- AI posture and attack-pressure behavior
- map-control and contested-resource incentives
- transport-layer and runtime validation thinking
- documentation structure for iterative game R&D

## Final framing

LW2B should be remembered as a successful small RTS demo and research vehicle.

Its archival state is a deliberate completion decision, not an abandoned half-step.
